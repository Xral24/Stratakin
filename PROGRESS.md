Working copy: /home/claude/strata (mirrored to this repo's src/ before each commit). Build: `python3 tools/build.py`. Tests/screenshots: /home/claude/strata-test (harness.js), `node shot.js <seed> <out.png>`.

## Architecture decisions

1. **Entity geometry** (`js/geo.js` + MeshBuilder in render.js): entity meshes carry smooth normals and a bone index per vertex. New primitives: `blob` (box-to-ellipsoid with taper), `tube` (path + radii, caps), `fin` (extruded polygon), `box` (kept). A transform stack (`push/rotate/pop`) and `setBone(i)`.
2. **Entity shader** (render.js `EVS/EFS`): aPos/aCol/aNorm/aBone, `uBones[10]` matrices, soft toon ramp + rim light, `uSway` for serpents/fish, optional inverted-hull outline. Chunks keep the old shader.
3. **Bones**: 0 body, 1 head, 2 front-left leg / left arm, 3 front-right / right arm, 4 back-left leg, 5 back-right leg, 6 tail, 7 wing L, 8 wing R, 9 jaw. `model.bones[i] = {piv:[x,y,z], kind}`. Pose is computed per frame in render.js from how far the entity moved (`e._an`), or from `e.anim` when the producer supplies it (player, mount).
4. **Portraits**: rendered with GL into an offscreen FBO and copied to the 2D canvas (`R.renderPortrait`), so they match the in-game look.
5. **Model parts** keep `{x,y,z,w,h,d,c,emis}` boxes for backward compatibility (decorations, hats); new parts add `k: 'blob'|'tube'|'fin'` with their own fields. `KIN.buildMesh` dispatches.

## Models from files, slash trails (Nov 22, Alexander away, no credits spent)
- **GLB loader** (`js/glb.js`): reads binary glTF (what Higgsfield makes): mesh, normals, texture coordinates, the embedded base colour texture (decoded in the background, shrunk to 1K on phones), the skin and the animation clips. A skeleton with more than 14 joints is folded into the shader's 14 bone groups by how much of the mesh each joint carries; each vertex follows its two strongest groups, blended (new `aBone2` attribute), so elbows and knees bend. Models are normalised to stand on y = 0 at a given height, facing +z. `GLB.CAST` names who wears a file model (Finch is listed with `file: null` until his file exists); `GLB.dressAll` dresses them by name as they are drawn and nods the head while they talk. Their box models stay for portraits and everything else.
- **Shaders:** entity shader takes `aUV`/`uTex`/`uUseTex` and a second blended bone; untextured meshes are unchanged (UV -1, weight 0). Shadows use the same pose.
- **Tests:** `tools/test/make_test_glb.py` builds a skinned, textured, animated test GLB (validated with the Khronos validator, 0 errors); `t-glb.js` loads it and plays clips; `t-glb2.js` checks Finch at the Starwell wears a file model.
- **Slimming:** `tools/art/slim.py in.glb out.glb [tris] [tex]`: gltfpack cuts to a triangle budget (40k-triangle test ball to 3,979, 1.1 MB to 73 KB), PIL shrinks textures to JPEG/PNG. Output validated and loaded in the game.
- **Slash trails** (`Combat.slashEntity`): a glowing crescent swept in front of you on each swing, drawn additively; sword sweeps across (alternating, slightly tilted), axe chops on a diagonal, spear leaves a flat streak; colour follows the blade (fire orange, frost blue, pierce violet, all gold). ## The cast redone at the phone's ceiling, walking fixed, tyrants back to Kin (Nov 22, evening)
- Alexander called the first cast models poor, and he was right: they were Tripo H3.1 on its cheapest settings (10k faces, standard texture) and the phone cut their pictures to 1024. Worked out as 2048 textures (a character fills at most about 1,000 pixels of a phone screen, so 2048 is already past one texel a pixel; 4096 would cost four times the memory for nothing visible) and about 40,000 triangles a character.

- **Second clips.** `tools/art/strip_skin.py` takes the rig off a GLB (mesh as it stands at rest, texture kept), so the same mesh can be rigged again for another clip (8). Hask got Thoughtful_Walk (his old Elderly_Shaky_Walk shook by design, half of why he "vibrated"), Stonewall got Idle_11 to stand in. - **Walking fixed** (he tried it: too fast, shivering, worst with the key):
  - A Meshy walk clip holds two to four steps, and the old code played one whole clip per stride, so legs ran three times too fast. The clip plays at real time when he moves at that speed (clamped 0.55 to 1.6 of it otherwise).
  - People in a model move at their model's pace: village wanderers get `speed = v0`, a tyrant's patrol period is stretched to it, and Hask's scripted walks (`elderWalk`) use 1.5 and 1.3 times his pace instead of 2.6 and 2.2 m/s (his wait before he gives up walking to you went from 7 to 12 s to match). `GLB.pace(o)` gives the number.

## The world's look, first pass: storybook light (Nov 22, night)
His ask: "the world needs to be done". Measured against `art/style` (the village and the countryside pictures) the game was too green, too blue and too hard: grass (0.29, 0.61, 0.15) against the picture's (0.6, 0.57, 0.33), a shadow on a roof at 0.36 of the lit side against about 0.65, cobbles neutral grey against warm sandstone.
- **A grade in the one tonemap every shader shares** (`TONE` in `render.js`, uniform `uGrade`): shadows open and lean violet, lights go warm, the top end is held back. `state.grade` is 1 by day on Earth, 0.6 on the Halo, 0 on the airless Moon and at night, less under a hold's gloom. Every program that tonemaps is in `GRADED` and is told the value at the top of `render`.
- **Softer sun, fuller shade**: by `uGrade`, the sky's fill light is up about a third and the sun down a little, in the terrain, entity and grass shaders. Shade to lit on a roof went from 0.36 to about 0.55.
- **Sky**: a paler noon, a zenith that stays blue, and on Earth the horizon pales to warm cream instead of white-blue. Earth's high sun is honey (1, 0.93, 0.78).
- **Grass**: the biome tints are meadow green with the sun in it (forest and plains moved from lawn green toward yellow-green); the grass block's own colour too.
- **Thatch**: browner straw, rows whose butt ends shadow the row below (reads as layers from down the street), and patches of old brown and new gold.
- **Cobbles**: sandstone and grey together, every stone its own, moss and grass in the joints in patches.
- Test `t-look2.js`: the village square and the open country, phone portrait, HUD hidden, `ROOT=` to shoot an older copy for a side by side.
- Not yet done (next): tree leaves are still lime; plaster and timber could take more warmth and grime; flowers, stone walls and a proper fountain in the first village; painterly clouds; water; then the Moon and the Halo, which have their own pictures (`2-moon-town`, `4-halo-town`).
